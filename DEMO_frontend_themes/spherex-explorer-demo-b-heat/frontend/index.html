How a frame gets to your screen

Every view asks the public archives at request time, through the server's cache. Nothing is invented and nothing outside the recorded snapshot is ever substituted.

  1. Step 1

    Find the images that cover your target

    An IVOA SIA2 query to IRSA returns every Wide and Deep Survey image from QR2 and QR3 that overlaps the position.

  2. Step 2

    Cut out a small region

    Each image is 2040 × 2040 pixels per detector, so the app asks the cutout service, or reads byte ranges from the S3 mirror, instead of downloading whole files.

  3. Step 3

    Read the wavelength at the target

    The per-pixel wavelength comes from the image's spectral WCS, so every frame is labelled with what the target was actually seen at.

Two releases, one timeline

Quick Releases 2 and 3 are consecutive slices of time, not reprocessings of the same data, so the app queries both. QR3 uses new calibrations, and the SPHEREx team advises caution when combining them.
